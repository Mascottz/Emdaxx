What to check before buying a used laptop

A used laptop can be a practical choice for work, school or a home office. The important question is not simply whether it turns on, but whether its condition and specifications match what you need it to do.

Start with the work you actually do

List your essential applications, the files you handle and whether you need to carry the laptop regularly. Check the processor, memory and storage against the requirements of your software. A machine for documents and browsing may have different needs from one used for design or video editing.

Inspect the parts you touch

Look closely at the hinges, casing and screen. Open and close the lid gently, test the keyboard and touchpad, and check the webcam, speakers and wireless connection. Plug into available ports where possible. Cosmetic wear is not automatically a problem, but cracks around hinges or a loose charging socket deserve a closer look.

Ask about the battery and charger

Battery life varies with age and use. Ask about its condition, check that the charger connects securely, and find out whether a replacement battery is available for that model. A laptop that works only when plugged in may still be useful, but the price and your expectations should reflect that.

Check access and ownership

Make sure the seller can remove their accounts and that the device is not locked to an organisation or previous owner. Ask about its history, any repairs and what happens if a fault is discovered after purchase. Never rely on appearance alone.

Make an informed choice

Compare the total cost, including any upgrades or repairs you expect to need. If you are unsure, ask Emdax about current computer availability and what to check for your particular use.
